Why Google Analytics on WordPress creates problems

WordPress powers 40% of the web, but GA4 was not designed with WordPress privacy requirements in mind.

Google Analytics requires cookie consent on WordPress

Installing GA4 on a WordPress site means a consent popup for every visitor. Under GDPR, you can't set GA4 cookies without explicit opt-in. That banner costs you conversions and corrupts your data.

GA4 sends data to US servers

Google Analytics transfers visitor data to US servers. For European sites, this creates legal exposure under SCHREMS II — a risk WordPress site owners increasingly can't ignore.

WordPress analytics plugins are bloated

Most GA4 WordPress plugins add significant weight to your site. Page speed suffers, Core Web Vitals scores drop, and you're still dealing with cookies.

You can't see user behavior, only pageviews

GA4 tells you how many people visited. OpenPanel tells you what they did: which CTAs they clicked, where they dropped off, which blog posts convert to signups.
